Applicable Oregon Law

  • Oregon Unlawful Trade Practices Act (ORS 646.608): Classifying a manufacturing defect as customer-caused damage without diagnostic evidence, then using that classification to deny warranty coverage, may constitute an unfair or deceptive practice under ORS 646.608(1)(s). Representing that warranty exclusions apply when the factual basis for those exclusions has not been established may violate ORS 646.608(1)(e). Additionally, ORS 646.608(1)(t) makes it unlawful to fail to disclose "any known material defect or material nonconformity" concurrent with delivery. The Playdate's design violates two separate specifications from Sharp's own datasheet for the LS027B7DH01 display: Handling Note 11 warns against placing adhesive on the panel surface (the Playdate bonds the LCD to the housing with adhesive), and Operating Instruction 3 warns that still images should not be displayed for more than two hours (the Playdate displays a static clock face indefinitely while charging). These design nonconformities were not disclosed to consumers.
  • Oregon Implied Warranty of Merchantability (ORS 72.3140 / 72.8020): Consumer goods must be fit for ordinary use. Panic's warranty does not include a conspicuous "as is" disclaimer (ORS 72.8050), so the implied warranty applies alongside the express warranty.
  • Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. 2301-2312): Panic provides a written "Limited Warranty" on a consumer product exceeding $25, triggering federal protections. The implied warranty cannot be disclaimed. Under §2304(c), a warrantor may only waive warranty obligations if it "can show" the failure was caused by consumer damage "not resulting from defect or malfunction." Panic has not made such a showing.

Desired Resolution

I am not seeking a replacement or refund for my individual unit. I am filing this complaint because Panic's warranty evaluation process appears to systematically misclassify progressive LCD manufacturing defects as "impact damage" without producing diagnostic evidence. I am asking the Oregon AG's office to:

  1. Review whether Panic's practice of denying warranty claims based on visual impression alone, without diagnostic testing or identification of a specific impact point, constitutes an unfair trade practice under ORS 646.608
  2. Ensure that other Oregon consumers and out-of-state customers purchasing from this Oregon company receive evidence-based warranty evaluations

Complaint Description

I purchased a Panic Playdate ($229 handheld game console) that developed a progressive screen defect while sitting on my desk on a charger. This device was never dropped, struck, or impacted. I am the only person who used it.

On June 8, 2026, I contacted Panic support with photos documenting the screen issue. Support representative Amy Forbes described the display pattern as "odd."

On July 30, 2026, Amy approved a warranty replacement (RMA) and sent a prepaid shipping label. She made this decision after reviewing my photos. I shipped the unit back.

On August 14, 2026, after receiving my unit, Amy reversed the warranty approval. She claimed the screen showed "impact damage" and offered to either return the unit at my expense or provide a 30% discount coupon on a new purchase.

On August 18, 2026, Amy wrote:

"I approved the RMA because you claimed the unit had not been damaged, but as the photo shows, there has been impact damage to the screen at some point."

She also admitted:

"I was trying to solicit more information without the implication that you were not being truthful. We have had many customers claim that nothing has ever happened to their Playdate, we approve the RMA, then, once it arrives, we see that there was an undisclosed non-manufacturer's issue with the unit."

Evidence Against Impact

The defect changed between my pre-shipping photos and Panic's post-receipt photo. Before shipping, the entire screen showed dense vertical line artifacts. After Panic received the unit, the screen showed a localized dark diagonal streak in the lower left. Impact damage creates a fixed pattern at the moment of force. A defect that transforms over weeks is consistent with progressive LCD failure, not impact.

There is no physical evidence of impact on the unit: no cracking, no case damage, no dent, no scuff, no identifiable point of contact. The Playdate's display is glued directly to the housing with no protective frame (iFixit teardown), making it susceptible to adhesive degradation and thermal stress over time.

Panic has not provided any diagnostic report, technician assessment, or identification of a specific impact point. The denial appears to rest entirely on a visual impression of a photograph, combined with a generalized assumption that customers are untruthful about device handling.

Additionally, the Playdate's design violates two separate specifications from Sharp's own datasheet for the display panel it uses (LS027B7DH01): one warning against placing adhesive on the panel surface, and another limiting static image display to two hours. Both violations create conditions for the exact type of progressive LCD failure documented in this unit. These design nonconformities were not disclosed to consumers at the point of sale.

Systemic Concern

Amy's statement about "many customers" suggests this is a pattern, not an isolated judgment. If Panic routinely classifies progressive LCD failure as "impact damage" based on visual impression and then denies warranty claims, consumers without the technical background to challenge the diagnosis are being denied coverage they are entitled to under both Panic's express warranty and Oregon's implied warranty of merchantability.
