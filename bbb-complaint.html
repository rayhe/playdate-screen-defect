BBB Complaint: Panic, Inc.

This complaint is being filed with the Better Business Bureau (BBB Great West + Pacific region). It concerns Panic's practice of classifying progressive LCD manufacturing defects as "impact damage" to deny warranty coverage.

Desired Resolution

I am not seeking a replacement or refund for my individual unit. I am filing this complaint because Panic's warranty diagnosis process misclassifies progressive LCD manufacturing defects as "impact damage" without diagnostic evidence. I want Panic to:

  1. Review its warranty evaluation process for screen defect claims
  2. Stop classifying progressive LCD failure as "impact damage" without identifying an actual point of impact or producing a diagnostic report
  3. Ensure other customers with similar claims receive fair, evidence-based evaluations

Complaint Description

I purchased a Panic Playdate ($229 handheld game console) that developed a progressive screen defect while sitting on my desk on a charger. This device was never dropped, struck, or impacted. I am the only person who used it.

On June 8, 2026, I contacted Panic support with photos documenting the screen issue. On June 10, support representative Amy Forbes described the pattern as "odd."

On July 30, 2026, Amy approved a warranty replacement (RMA) and sent me a prepaid shipping label. I shipped the unit back in reliance on that approval.

On August 14, 2026, after receiving my unit, Amy reversed the warranty approval. She claimed the screen showed "impact damage" and offered to either ship the unit back at my expense or provide a 30% discount coupon.

On August 18, 2026, Amy doubled down:

"I approved the RMA because you claimed the unit had not been damaged, but as the photo shows, there has been impact damage to the screen at some point."

She also admitted:

"I was trying to solicit more information without the implication that you were not being truthful. We have had many customers claim that nothing has ever happened to their Playdate, we approve the RMA, then, once it arrives, we see that there was an undisclosed non-manufacturer's issue with the unit."

This admission reveals that Amy (1) suspected damage from my original photos, (2) approved the RMA anyway, (3) reversed based on the same evidence she already had, and (4) is applying a population-level heuristic ("many customers claim nothing happened") rather than diagnosing my specific unit.

Why This Is Not Impact Damage

The defect changed between my pre-shipping photos and the photo Panic took after receipt. Before shipping, the entire screen showed dense vertical line artifacts. After Panic received it, the screen showed a localized dark diagonal streak in the lower left. Impact creates a fixed pattern at the moment of contact. This transformation over weeks is consistent with progressive LCD failure, not impact.

There is no physical evidence of impact: no cracking, no case damage, no dent or scuff on the housing, no impact point on the screen. The Playdate's display is glued directly to the housing with no protective frame, as documented in iFixit's teardown, making it inherently susceptible to adhesive stress over time.

Panic has not provided any diagnostic report, technician assessment, or identification of a specific point of impact. The "impact damage" conclusion appears to be based entirely on a visual impression.

Pattern of Concern

Amy's own words suggest this is not an isolated evaluation. Her statement about "many customers" claiming nothing happened implies Panic routinely applies this assumption to deny claims. If they are classifying progressive LCD failure as "impact damage" based on visual impression alone, customers without the technical background to challenge the diagnosis are being unfairly denied coverage.
